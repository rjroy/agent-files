# Evidence-backed workflow comparison recommendation

## Key findings

Retain the current OpenCode instruction-and-artifact workflow as the operational workflow. Beads remains the authority for task and dependency state, Git remains the source authority, and independent review remains required. The documentary record does not establish a supported, loaded, or effective OpenCode replacement. The dated, source-supported baseline and lifecycle record remains available as [baseline evidence](workflow-comparison-baseline-evidence.md); it is retained evidence, not a current-host claim.

The only bounded mechanisms worth preparing for a later, explicitly authorized decision are Forge's evidence validation and Factory's dry-run admission decision. They have directly inspectable source evidence, but no OpenCode registration or host-load evidence. They are adaptation candidates, not adoption work. Do not transfer Teamsters dispatch, Test Automation resume, Ponytail hooks, Open Wrapper, local-provider configuration, or a substantial replacement. No blanket trial is needed to make this recommendation.

Evidence grades use the [decision rubric](workflow-comparison-decision-rubric.md): B is inspectable source or checked-in tests, C is documentation, and D is unknown or conflicting. A source or test result does not prove OpenCode loading or runtime effectiveness.

## Coherent OpenCode recommendation

Do not adopt the marketplace as an OpenCode workflow. Keep the baseline and preserve its task-state, source-state, and review boundaries. Treat the two narrow source-backed mechanisms as separate future design options, not a package: Forge evidence validation first, Factory admission gating only if its added record solves a named admission problem. Do not combine them by default.

The strongest rejected alternative is a full replacement. It promises broad orchestration, dispatch, recovery, hooks, and provider behavior, but the documented evidence instead shows Claude-specific dependencies, multiple competing state stores, unverified upstream provenance, and no OpenCode host trace. Its claimed breadth does not offset its unsupported dependencies or maintenance cost.

No trial is a prerequisite for this documentary recommendation. If a later authorized decision depends on a specific claim, use one isolated smoke test per claim, under the rubric's safety, comparability, evidence-integrity, and independent-review gates.

Confine Forge writes to an approved disposable worktree. |

## Traceability, fairness, priority, and conflict review

**Traceability.** Every recommendation above links to a source artifact and retains the evidence grade rather than upgrading documentation to runtime proof. The capability assessment records unavailable upstream/release verification and no installation, configuration, import, trial, or live-service interaction. Those limits apply to this review.

**Fairness.** The baseline is retained as a first-class option. No alternative receives credit for a feature outside its declared boundary, and no source presence, README, manifest, or checked-in test is treated as a host-load result. This follows the rubric's common-condition and gate rules.

**Priority.** The order is safety and authoritative state, then correct and independently reviewed completion, then optional maintainability improvements. This applies the [decision rubric's gates and decision rules](workflow-comparison-decision-rubric.md#gates), which make safety and OpenCode feasibility non-tradeable gates and prevent time, cost, or feature breadth from compensating for a gap.

**Conflicts and missing evidence.** The material identifies potential disagreement among Beads, Forge, Factory, `.lore/work/notes`, and Claude-specific state. It also identifies unsupported dispatch/hooks, no verified upstream provenance or current maintenance, no OpenCode load trace, no migration or rollback design, and no runtime effectiveness evidence. These omissions support retention and deferral, not adoption.
