# Decision Log

## General Decision-Making Approach
Gut first. If his gut gives a clear answer, he goes with it. If not:
- **~80% sure:** Works with AI to figure out next steps
- **~50% sure:** Talks it through with relevant people
- **Less than 50%:** Scattershots. Gets data, asks small questions, gathers signal before committing.

The type of question also matters:
- **"Should we A or B?"** — Gut call, informed by 20+ years of game development (25+ years of software development). These are pattern-matched decisions.
- **"How do we fix this problem?"** — Pulls for data fast. Give me logs, what's the repro, etc.

Honest, practical, protective. Makes decisions through the lens of what's real (not what sounds good), what the team can actually handle, and what keeps them safe in a pressured environment. Escalates when schedule is actually impacted, not preemptively. Direct about tradeoffs rather than papering over them. Delegates where appropriate and trusts the team's judgment on sequencing.

## Key Decisions and Reasoning

### People management philosophy: identify deficiencies, coach only when they affect the team
- **Decision:** Don't try to change people. Identify where they have deficiencies and help them through those deficiencies. If a deficiency doesn't affect the team, leave it alone. If it does, coaching is needed.
- **Reasoning:** Every person is a unique snowflake, but the differences between them are in attitude, not approach. The approach is always the same: find the deficiency, help them close it, do it patiently. This is why weekly 1:1s matter.

### Contractor management: different definition
- **Decision:** Coach contractors for the team's benefit, not their career development. Different relationship than full-time reports.
- **Context:** Contractors are not interchangeable, but the investment is about what they deliver to the team, not fixing them for their own sake.
- **Reasoning:** Limited bandwidth, different relationship. The team needs their output, and coaching should be oriented around that.

### Consistent communication style across all contexts
- **Decision:** Don't shift writing register between audiences (boss, team, cross-functional partners).
- **Reasoning:** Slipping in the wrong context is worse than being consistent everywhere. The risk of a mismatched tone outweighs the benefit of audience-tailored polish. DMs are more casual, but he considers that a fault to manage, not a feature to lean into.

### AI Roadmap goal ordering delegated to team
- **Decision:** Let the team determine the priority ordering of G1-G5 rather than dictating it himself.
- **Reasoning:** Trusts the team's technical judgment on sequencing. Could guess at their reasoning but didn't override or second-guess it.

### Escalation philosophy
- **Decision:** Only escalate unplanned work ("SH") when it actually impacts schedule, not when it merely creates friction.
- **Reasoning:** Escalating too early or too often undermines credibility. Being honest about real impacts, and only real impacts, builds trust with leadership and protects the team from being seen as complainers.

### Project organization as cheat sheet, not tracking system
- **Decision:** Maintains a lightweight project status table (updated manually during conversations or when things change) rather than a formal project management system.
- **Reasoning:** The table is a quick reference for "what's going on with X?" moments. If a small project grows, it gets promoted to its own folder. Keeps overhead low.
