## Industry Context & Organizational Realities (The "Insider" View)
- **Seasonal Pressure:** Q4 (Oct-Dec) is the absolute critical shipping window for the game industry.
- **Legacy Title Decay:** Flagship titles will eventually face natural audience decline, which creates massive but silent organizational pressure to pivot resources.
- **Organizational Contraction:** In times of market volatility or layoffs, "visible, reliable delivery" is the primary currency for team survival. Technical excellence matters less than shipping on time.
- **The Unplanned Work Gap:** Constant reactive work (fires, unplanned incidents) is the silent killer of high-value initiatives. Managers must protect the team's focus from it.
- **Platform vs. Raw Implementation:** Internal platform/SDK teams face a recurring existential argument: product teams can "just build/make direct calls" themselves, so platform teams must constantly justify their added value.
- **Platform Adoption is Political:** The threat of bypassing a platform is political, not technical. Individual engineers can be won over with "we're trying to help," but leadership-level concerns require executive escalation.
- **Launcher/Storefront Architecture:** Understanding the complex relationship between central launchers, storefronts, and the core game client.
- **Gamedev Overwork Normalization:** Industry work-life balance expectations are incredibly poor. It often feels "wrong" to take normal vacation days, but protecting that time is the manager's job.
- **Contractor Investment Models:** Contractors have a different management contract: coaching is oriented around immediate team output, not their long-term career development.

## Frameworks / Mental Models
- **Positive-Constructive-Positive Feedback (The "Sandwich"):** Start with what is working, define the specific deviation, and show how their *existing* strengths can solve the problem. Used with reports who need behavioral correction.
- **"You can't change a person, but you can train them":** A patient, iterative approach to managing difficult personalities. Small adjustments over time, not ultimatums.
- **Attitude vs. Approach:** Every person is unique, but the differences between them are in their *attitude*, not their *approach*. The management approach is the same: find the deficiency, help them through it, be patient. Weekly 1:1s are the primary mechanism.
- **Coach only when it affects the team:** If a deficiency doesn't impact the team's output or culture, leave it alone. Coaching is for gaps that create real problems, not for making people conform to a specific mold.
- **"Projecting myself onto them":** Recognizing younger versions of yourself in direct reports. The instinct is to preach what you know now, but they won't hear it until they need to. Instead, name specific consequences and let their experience do the teaching.
- **Make implications visible:** Instead of blocking a choice (e.g., taking vacation, or taking on too much scope), show the inevitable consequences and let them decide the impact. "If you do this, you will miss X deadline, and you will need to escalate to the VP. Do you want to do that?" Let them see the radar they'd be on.
- **"Baking time":** Some people just need time and experience to mature. Not every deficiency has a coaching solution; some require patience and exposure to the right mistakes.
