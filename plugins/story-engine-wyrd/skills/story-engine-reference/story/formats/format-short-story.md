# Format: Short Story

## Format Specifications

**Word Count:** 1,000-5,000 words
**Read Time:** 5-25 minutes
**Structure:** Tight three-act arc, focused scope

---

## Core Characteristics

### 1. Focused Scope

**Short stories work best with:**
- Single protagonist (or tight pair)
- One primary conflict
- Limited time span (hours to weeks, not years)
- 2-4 key scenes
- Focused theme or question

**Avoid:**
- Multiple plot threads
- Large cast of characters
- Epic scope or world-building
- Extended time periods requiring summary
- Complex subplots

### 2. Complete Arc

**Unlike flash (single moment) or epic (extended journey), short stories have:**
- Clear beginning, middle, end
- Character change or revelation
- Conflict established and resolved
- Satisfying conclusion

**Structure balance:**
- Tight enough to maintain momentum
- Roomy enough to develop character and world
- Long enough for emotional investment
- Short enough to read in one sitting

### 3. Efficiency With Depth

**Short stories must:**
- Establish quickly but with richness
- Develop character through action, not extensive interiority
- Build world through specific details, not explanation
- Create emotional resonance efficiently

---

## Short Story Structure

### Act 1: Setup (20-25%)

**Word Count:** 200-1250 words (depending on total length)

**Elements to Establish:**
- **Protagonist** - Who they are, what they want
- **World** - Enough detail to ground reader
- **Status quo** - Normal life before disruption
- **Inciting incident** - Event that starts story
- **Stakes** - What matters, what's at risk

**Pacing:**
- First paragraph: hook reader
- First page: establish protagonist and world
- End of Act 1: inciting incident changes everything

**Example (Telvaren Short Story):**
```
Pages 1-2: Protagonist's routine as Memory Archivist
Page 3: Discovers memory that contradicts official history
Page 4: Decision to investigate despite danger
```

### Act 2: Development (50-60%)

**Word Count:** 500-3000 words

**What Happens:**
- **Protagonist pursues goal** - Active investigation, action, choices
- **Obstacles escalate** - Each challenge harder than last
- **Midpoint** - Major revelation or reversal (around 50%)
- **Complications** - Internal and external conflicts intensify
- **Dark moment** - All seems lost, character at lowest point

**Scene Structure:**
- 2-4 major scenes
- Each scene has purpose (plot or character development)
- Balance action with reflection
- Vary pacing (fast/slow rhythm)

**Midpoint Function:**
- New information reframes understanding
- Character makes significant choice
- Stakes increase dramatically
- Story shifts direction

**Example (Telvaren):**
```
Scene 1: Interview witnesses, gather evidence
Scene 2: Discover protagonist's own memories might be edited
Midpoint: Realize authority figure is behind memory edits
Scene 3: Attempt to preserve evidence, narrowly escape
Dark Moment: Evidence destroyed, no proof remains
```

### Act 3: Resolution (15-25%)

**Word Count:** 150-1250 words

**Elements:**
- **Climax** - Final confrontation or revelation
- **Resolution** - Consequences play out
- **Character change** - Growth demonstrated
- **Thematic statement** - Story's meaning crystallizes
- **Ending** - Satisfying closure (or meaningful ambiguity)

**Pacing:**
- Build to climactic moment
- Brief denouement (don't linger too long)
- Strong final image or line

**Example (Telvaren):**
```
Climax: Protagonist chooses to preserve truth in their own memory, knowing they'll be edited/erased
Resolution: World shifts, protagonist becomes "forgotten" but truth survives in them
Final Image: Alone but certain, guardian of what really happened
```

## Scene Construction for Short Stories

### Scene Requirements

**Each scene should:**
- Advance plot OR develop character (ideally both)
- Have clear purpose in overall arc
- Build toward climax
- Vary in pacing/tone from surrounding scenes

### Scene Types

**Discovery Scenes**
- Protagonist learns crucial information
- Raises questions, creates urgency
- Often Act 1 or early Act 2

**Confrontation Scenes**
- Character faces obstacle or antagonist
- Conflict externalized
- Tests character's values/abilities

**Reflection Scenes**
- Character processes events
- Internal struggle revealed
- Slower pacing, emotional depth

**Revelation Scenes**
- Truth comes to light
- Reframes earlier events
- Often midpoint or climax

**Action Scenes**
- Fast-paced, high tension
- Physical conflict or urgent choices
- Sparse description, sharp dialogue

### Balancing Scenes

**Short stories typically:**
- 3-5 total scenes
- Mix of action/reflection
- Build in intensity
- Vary pacing for rhythm

**Transitions between scenes:**
- Time jumps (hours to days)
- Summary of unimportant events
- New location or situation
- White space for breathing room

## Character in Short Stories

### Protagonist Depth

**Limited words require efficiency:**

**Reveal character through:**
- Specific details (one vivid trait beats three generic ones)
- Choices under pressure
- Distinctive voice
- Relationships (how they treat others)
- Reactions to world

**Avoid:**
- Extensive backstory dumps
- Long internal monologues
- Describing every aspect of personality
- Generic "everyman" protagonists

**Example (Efficient Character Establishment):**
```
She counted the artifacts three times before opening. Always three—
once for the inventory, once for the truth, once because she couldn't
stop herself. Ten years as an Archivist and she still didn't trust
her own memory. In Telvaren, that wasn't paranoia. That was survival.
```

**This establishes:**
- Profession (Archivist)
- Personality (careful, compulsive)
- Fear (can't trust memory)
- World context (Telvaren's nature)
- Experience (ten years)

### Supporting Characters

**In short stories, supporting characters should:**
- Serve clear function (ally, antagonist, foil)
- Have 1-2 distinctive traits
- Not steal focus from protagonist
- Interact meaningfully with protagonist

**Avoid:**
- Large casts
- Characters who serve no purpose
- Backstories for minor characters

---

## Dialogue in Short Stories

### Efficiency

**Dialogue must:**
- Reveal character quickly
- Advance plot or create conflict
- Sound distinctive per character
- No filler conversation

**Cut:**
- Greetings (unless character-revealing)
- Small talk
- Repetitive information
- Lengthy explanations

### Subtext

**Use implication:**
- Characters avoid saying important things directly
- Actions contradict words
- What's unsaid matters

**Example:**
```
"Did you edit it?" she asked.
He looked away. "Does it matter?"
"Answer the question."
"Some things are better forgotten."
"That's not an answer."
```

**Subtext:** He did edit it, feels guilty, justifies it, she knows.

---

## Pacing for Short Stories

### Opening: Fast

- Hook in first paragraph
- Establish protagonist/world quickly
- Get to inciting incident promptly
- No slow burn openings

### Middle: Varied

- Alternate fast (action/urgency) and slow (reflection/atmosphere)
- Build tension through escalation
- Midpoint often shifts pace
- Dark moment slows before final acceleration

### Ending: Earned Resolution

- Climax is fast, intense
- Brief denouement
- Don't linger unnecessarily
- Strong final beat

## Short Story Quality Checklist

### Structure

✓ Clear three-act structure
✓ Inciting incident within first 20-25%
✓ Midpoint revelation/reversal
✓ Climax delivers on setup
✓ Satisfying resolution

### Scope

✓ Single protagonist focus
✓ One primary conflict
✓ Limited time span
✓ Manageable cast
✓ Focused theme

### Efficiency

✓ No wasted scenes
✓ Every scene advances story
✓ Dialogue is tight, purposeful
✓ Character revealed through action
✓ World established through detail, not explanation

### Impact

✓ Character changes or learns
✓ Conflict resolves meaningfully
✓ Thematic resonance
✓ Ending earned through arc

## Short Story + World/Lens/Theory Integration

### With Worlds

**Short stories can:**
- Develop world through protagonist's interaction with it
- Focus on one aspect of world mechanics
- Use world to create unique conflict
- Imply larger world through specific details

**Telvaren Example:**
Focus on memory editing as relationship test—don't try to explore all world aspects.

### With Lenses

**Lens provides structure:**
- **Mystery:** Investigation arc fits perfectly
- **Romance:** Condensed relationship development
- **Horror:** Sustained dread building to climax
- **Coming-of-age:** Single transformative experience

### With Theory

**Narrative Structure:**
- Full three-act arc
- Clear conflict escalation
- Midpoint and climax distinct

**Character:**
- Protagonist has clear arc
- Supporting characters serve functions
- Relationships develop efficiently

**Prose:**
- Balance showing vs. telling
- Varied pacing through sentence structure
- Sensory details create immersion

**Dialogue:**
- Character-revealing exchanges
- Subtext in important conversations
- Efficient information delivery

## Common Short Story Mistakes

### Problem: Scope Too Large

**Symptom:** Trying to fit epic plot in 3,000 words

**Solution:**
- Focus on single conflict
- Limited time span
- One protagonist journey
- Imply larger world, don't explain it all

### Problem: Slow Start

**Symptom:** First 500 words are setup before anything happens

**Solution:**
- Start closer to inciting incident
- Weave setup into action
- Hook in first paragraph

### Problem: Rushed Ending

**Symptom:** Story resolves too quickly or conveniently

**Solution:**
- Ensure climax is earned
- Give brief denouement
- Don't solve everything in last paragraph

### Problem: Underdeveloped Characters

**Symptom:** Protagonist feels generic or unmotivated

**Solution:**
- Specific details over generic traits
- Clear desires and fears
- Distinctive voice
- Choices that reveal character

---

## Final Principles for Short Stories

1. **Focused scope** - One protagonist, one conflict, limited time
2. **Complete arc** - Beginning, middle, end with character change
3. **Efficient depth** - Quick establishment, meaningful development
4. **Purposeful scenes** - Every scene earns its place
5. **Tight dialogue** - No filler, all character-revealing
6. **Strong structure** - Clear acts, midpoint, climax
7. **Resonant ending** - Satisfying closure or meaningful ambiguity

Short stories are the sweet spot between flash's compression and epic's expansiveness—**long enough for emotional investment and character depth, short enough to maintain tight focus and read in one sitting**.
