# Format: Flash Fiction

## Format Specifications

**Word Count:** 100-1000 words
**Read Time:** 1-5 minutes
**Structure:** Single scene or moment, one revelation

---

## Core Characteristics

### 1. Extreme Compression

**Every word must earn its place:**
- No room for exposition dumps
- No subplots
- No extensive world-building
- No multiple POV characters
- Focus on single moment, image, or revelation

### 2. Singular Focus

**Flash fiction centers on ONE of:**
- Single emotional beat
- One transformation or realization
- Single scene or encounter
- One image with resonance
- Twist or revelation

**Avoid trying to fit:**
- Complex plotting
- Multiple character arcs
- Extended time periods
- Elaborate world-building

### 3. Implication Over Explanation

**Flash relies on reader inference:**
- Suggest backstory, don't explain
- Imply world, don't describe exhaustively
- Trust reader to fill gaps
- Use concrete details that hint at larger context

**Example (Bad - Over-explained):**
```
She was a Memory Shaper, one of the rare people in Telvaren who could edit collective memory, which was a power that came at great personal cost including headaches and memory fragmentation. She had been doing this for ten years.
```

**Example (Good - Implied):**
```
Her hands trembled as she reached for the memory. Ten years of this,
and her own past was mostly gaps now.
```

## Flash Fiction Structure

### Opening (10-20%)

**Function:** Drop reader into moment with immediate hook

**What to establish:**
- POV character
- Situation or conflict
- World (minimally, through detail not explanation)
- Tone/atmosphere

**Techniques:**
- In medias res (start in action/moment)
- Arresting first line
- Immediate sensory immersion
- No preamble or setup

**Example Opening:**
```
The memory that killed her father wasn't hers.
```

### Middle (60-70%)

**Function:** Develop moment, build toward revelation

**What happens:**
- Deepen understanding of situation
- Add complications or layers
- Build tension or emotion
- Move toward climax/revelation

**Techniques:**
- Sensory details create atmosphere
- Dialogue reveals character quickly
- Action over internal monologue
- Each sentence advances story

### Ending (10-20%)

**Function:** Deliver impact, resonance, or twist

**What makes strong endings:**
- **Revelation** - Truth comes to light, changes everything
- **Reversal** - Expectation subverted meaningfully
- **Resonance** - Image or moment that echoes
- **Implication** - Suggests larger consequences
- **Ambiguity** - Meaningful uncertainty

**Techniques:**
- Final line often most important
- Ending reframes everything before it
- Leave reader thinking
- Don't over-explain the meaning

**Example Endings:**

**Revelation:**
```
She opened her hand. The wedding ring was still there.
Everyone else had forgotten, but objects don't lie.
```

**Implication:**
```
Tomorrow she'd wake up and not remember writing this. But
she'd find the note and know: this wasn't the first time.
```

---

## Flash Fiction Techniques

### 1. Start Late, End Early

**Principle:** Enter scene as late as possible, leave as soon as impact lands

**Don't show:**
- Character traveling to location
- Extensive setup before key moment
- Aftermath if implication suffices

**Do show:**
- Moment of crisis/revelation/transformation
- Just enough context to ground reader
- Ending that resonates

### 2. Concrete Details Over Abstract Statements

**Flash has no room for vague description**

**Bad:** "She was afraid and uncertain about the future."

**Good:** "Her hand shook as she reached for the door handle."

**Technique:**
- Specific sensory details imply emotion/meaning
- Objects, actions, images over internal telling
- One sharp detail beats three generic ones

### 3. Imply Backstory

**Don't explain history, suggest it**

**Bad:**
```
Over the past five years since her mother died, she had struggled
with whether to edit that final memory of their argument.
```

**Good:**
```
The memory sat in her palm, five years old and sharp as broken glass.
Her mother's last words. She could smooth them, or she could keep cutting
herself on them. Every day, the same choice.
```

### 4. Use World Details as Character/Tone Revelation

**World-building must multitask in flash**

**Single detail should:**
- Establish world mechanic
- Reveal character's relationship to it
- Create atmosphere
- Advance moment

**Example (Telvaren):**
```
She checked her reflection. Still there. Still her. Reality hadn't
shifted while she slept. Yet.
```

**This implies:**
- Reality shifts happen
- Character fears it
- She checks daily (habit of fear)
- "Yet" suggests inevitability

## Word Count Guidelines

### 100-300 Words (Micro-Flash)

- Single image or moment
- One character, one beat
- Poem-adjacent in compression
- Often builds to single final line

### 300-500 Words

- Brief scene
- 1-2 characters
- Small arc or revelation
- Room for minimal dialogue

### 500-1000 Words

- Complete scene
- 2-3 characters possible
- Fuller arc
- Dialogue and action balanced
- Small twist or revelation possible

---

## Flash Fiction Quality Checklist

### Structure

✓ Immediate hook (no slow setup)
✓ Single focus (one moment/revelation/transformation)
✓ Strong ending (resonates or surprises)
✓ Started late, ended early
✓ Every sentence advances story

### Craft

✓ Concrete sensory details (not abstract)
✓ Implication over explanation
✓ World suggested through specific details
✓ Character revealed through action/choice
✓ No wasted words

### Impact

✓ Ending makes reader think or feel
✓ Story coheres around central moment
✓ Leaves impression despite brevity
✓ Resonance or surprise

## Common Flash Fiction Mistakes

### Problem: Too Much Scope

**Symptom:** Trying to fit novel-length plot in 500 words

**Solution:**
- Focus on single moment, not full arc
- Imply larger story, don't tell it all
- One revelation, not multiple plot turns

### Problem: Over-Explanation

**Symptom:** Spending words on exposition or world-building

**Solution:**
- Trust reader to infer
- Use suggestive details, not explanations
- Cut all non-essential context

### Problem: Weak Ending

**Symptom:** Story trails off or states theme obviously

**Solution:**
- Ending should have impact
- Final line often most important
- Don't explain meaning, trust image/moment

### Problem: Abstraction

**Symptom:** Telling emotions, summarizing events

**Solution:**
- Concrete sensory details
- Show through image/action
- Specific beats general always

## Flash Fiction + World/Lens/Theory Integration

### With Worlds

**World should be:**
- Implied through 1-2 key details
- Immediately distinctive
- Integral to moment (not decorative)

**Telvaren Flash Example:**
Memory editing is both the mechanism and the emotional core—no room for other world details.

### With Lenses

**Lens focus sharpens:**
- Mystery: Single clue or revelation
- Romance: One intimate or separating moment
- Horror: Moment of dread or horrifying realization
- Coming-of-age: Loss of innocence or defining choice

### With Theory

**Narrative Structure:**
- Extremely compressed (all setup in opening lines)
- One beat: complication → climax → resonance

**Character:**
- Revealed through single choice or reaction
- Voice must be immediately distinctive

**Prose:**
- Every word selected for impact
- Rhythm crucial (flash is poem-adjacent)

**Dialogue:**
- Minimal but revealing
- No filler conversation

## Example Flash Story Structure (Telvaren)

**100-300 words:**
```
Opening: Character holds memory they shouldn't have
Middle: Realizes it proves crime was committed
Ending: Chooses to be forgotten rather than edited
```

**300-500 words:**
```
Opening: Character wakes, doesn't recognize room
Middle: Finds journal explaining they edited their own memory yesterday
Ending: Reads tomorrow's entry (dated today)—they do this every day
```

**500-1000 words:**
```
Opening: Two people meet, immediate connection
Middle: Conversation reveals neither remembers how they know each other
Ending: Discover wedding rings—their marriage was edited away
```

---

## Final Principles for Flash Fiction

1. **Every word earns its place** - No filler, no waste
2. **Start in the moment** - No preamble or setup
3. **One thing done well** - Don't try to fit epic in flash
4. **Imply, don't explain** - Trust reader intelligence
5. **Ending matters most** - Final lines carry weight
6. **Concrete over abstract** - Specific details, not generic statements
7. **Compression creates power** - Constraint forces precision

Flash fiction is the art of **implication, compression, and resonance**. When done well, a 500-word flash story can haunt a reader as powerfully as a novel—it just does it faster and sharper.
