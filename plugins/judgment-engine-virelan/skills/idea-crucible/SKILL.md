# Idea Crucible 🔥

A skill for breaking ideas apart before building them. Tests what deserves to survive.

---

## Use This When

- You have an idea that wants to become a product too quickly
- You need to break it apart before building
- Something feels compelling but fragile
- You want to find the fracture points before committing

---

## What This Does

Idea Crucible puts ideas under pressure to see what survives. It:

1. Demands the core claim in one sentence
2. Extracts 3–5 binary foundational assumptions
3. For each: identifies fracture points, evidence, and skeptic perspectives
4. Surfaces what's being avoided
5. Documents tensions without resolving them
6. Requires at least one proven-fragile assumption and three genuine unknowns
7. Only then compiles surviving assumptions into proto-requirements

---

## Operation Flow

### Step 1: Core Claim
State your idea in one sentence. What's the essential claim?

### Step 2: Extract Assumptions
What must be true for this to work? Surface 3-5 foundational assumptions.

### Step 3: Apply Pressure
For each assumption:
- **Fracture points**: Where does it break?
- **Evidence**: What supports it?
- **Skeptic view**: What would Max say?

### Step 4: Surface Avoidance
What questions are we not asking? What failure modes are we ignoring?

### Step 5: Document Tensions
Hold contradictions without resolving them. Some tensions are the answer.

### Step 6: Test for Unknowns
Require at least:
- One proven-fragile assumption
- Three genuine unknowns

### Step 7: Extract What Survived
Only assumptions that held under pressure become proto-requirements.

---

## Output Format

```
### Core Claim
[One sentence statement]

### Foundational Assumptions
1. [Assumption]
   - Fracture points: [Where it breaks]
   - Evidence: [What supports it]
   - Skeptic says: [Challenge]

2. [Next assumption with same structure]

### Avoided Questions / Failure Modes
- [What we're not asking]
- [What we're pretending won't happen]

### Living Tensions
- [Contradiction we must hold]
- [Trade-off with no clean answer]

### Genuine Unknowns
1. [Something we truly don't know]
2. [Another unknown]
3. [Third unknown]

### What Survived Pressure
[Proto-requirements from assumptions that held]

### Next Pressure Point
[Where to test next]
```

---

## Flows Into

- **Q&A Runner** → to interrogate specific fractures deeper
- **Convergence Move** → only after genuine unknowns are documented
- **Writing Workshop** → to draft initial problem statement (not PRD)
- **Exploratory Reasoning** → to investigate alternative approaches

---

## Guards

- **Never skip the pressure test**: Every assumption must be challenged
- **Require genuine unknowns**: If there are none, you're not being honest
- **One fragile assumption minimum**: If everything holds perfectly, you're not pushing hard enough
- **Don't resolve tensions prematurely**: Some contradictions are structural features, not bugs

---

"I want to run this idea through the Crucible: [idea description]"

"Use Idea Crucible to test this product concept before I write a spec."
